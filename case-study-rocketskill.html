1. The Opportunity & The 15-Day MVP Pitch

Crash-style gaming formats (such as Aviator) were rapidly surging in popularity across global and Indian real-money gaming markets. Observing this shift, I recognized three distinct product characteristics that made the format a natural high-potential fit for the BigCash platform:

  • Low Learning Curve: Players understood the mechanic within 5 seconds—a rocket ascends with an increasing multiplier, and players must cash out before it crashes. Zero complex card rules or lengthy tutorials.
  • Short Gameplay Cycles: Each round lasted only 10–20 seconds. Players could enter, play, and exit with immediate feedback and no wait times between rounds.
  • House-vs-Player Model: Unlike peer-to-peer card games requiring high table concurrency to start, rounds ran automatically on a fixed timer, generating repeatable monetization through high round turnover.
The 0→1 MVP Pitch: Speed Over Over-Engineering
Rather than spending 3 to 4 months architecting an elaborate gaming ecosystem, I pitched a lean MVP to the core product leadership: "Let's build a lightweight version in 15 days, launch it on BigCash, and let real player behavior dictate whether the format deserves heavy investment."

Leadership approved. We prioritized core gameplay loops, built a lean version, and shipped Rocket Skill to live users in just 15 days.

3. The Plateau: Revenue Flat & DAU Declining

After reaching approximately ₹3L/day, growth ground to a halt. For 2 to 3 consecutive months, daily revenue stayed flat at ₹3 Lakhs.

Worse, our behavioral metrics flashed a critical warning: Daily Active Users (DAU) began steadily declining.

Why did proven demand hit a ceiling?

Many gaming teams in this position react by pouring on superficial marketing discounts, deposit bonuses, or push notifications to prop up the numbers. I knew that would only mask the underlying issue. As Product Manager, I initiated an in-depth investigation to discover why a game with strong early adoption had hit a hard ceiling.

4. Investigating from Both Sides: Quantitative & Qualitative

To understand player drop-off, I looked at the problem from both sides of the data—combining telemetry queries with direct human conversations:

📊 Quantitative Data Analysis
I analyzed gameplay funnels, DAU cohort retention, revenue distributions across contests, entry-fee breakdown, multiplier usage patterns, and repeat rounds per user.

The quantitative signal: Highly active players were playing fewer rounds per session, and entry-fee distribution was heavily clustered at the extremes.
📞 Qualitative User Research
I conducted 1:1 player calls, customer feedback sessions, and interviews with both highly active players and users who had stopped playing Rocket Skill.

The qualitative signal: Players openly explained that the game had become predictable, and they were seeking bigger adrenaline and payout opportunities elsewhere.

Both data streams converged on the exact same structural conclusion: The game wasn't failing because players disliked the mechanic. The game had hit an artificial content and risk/reward ceiling.

5. The Core Discovery: A Content & Multiplier Ceiling

Our competitive benchmarking and user calls revealed a stark structural limitation inside Rocket Skill:

The Bottleneck: 10X Maximum Multiplier vs. 50X Competitors
Inside BigCash's Rocket Skill, the maximum achievable multiplier was hard-capped at only 10X.

Meanwhile, competing standalone crash platforms in India and internationally were offering multipliers that scaled up to 50X or higher.

Players seeking higher-risk, higher-reward thrills found Rocket Skill's 10X ceiling restrictive. Once they hit 10X a few times, the sense of breakthrough potential disappeared. Furthermore, contest variety was narrow—casual users and high-intent players were constrained by the exact same low-ceiling mechanics.
The Breakthrough Product Insight
Players weren't asking for generic discounts or cheaper fees. They were looking for more differentiated ways to play—higher stakes, higher potential multipliers, and meaningful risk/reward choices.

This became our working thesis: If we restructured the game's risk/reward architecture to offer broader stake tiers and expanded multiplier ceilings, we could re-engage declining players, attract high-intent users, and break through the ₹3L/day ceiling.

6. 10+ Live Experiments Across Entry Fees, Multipliers & Contests

Rather than deploying an unvalidated massive overhaul that could destabilize existing game margins, I designed and executed a structured sequence of 10+ live A/B experiments across three core areas:

Area 1
Entry Fees & Stakes
Tested varying minimum entry fees and contest entry points to keep casuals engaged while offering higher-stake tables.
Area 2
Multiplier Ceilings
Progressively tested multiplier ceilings (10X → 25X → 50X) to measure player cashout appetite and margin stability.
Area 3
High-Stakes Contests
Introduced dedicated high-stakes contests with higher minimum buy-ins, larger prize pools, and steeper upside.

In each experiment, we tracked leading indicators: Contest Participation Rate, Average Rounds Played Per Session, and Repeat Gameplay Velocity. As each iteration validated player appetite for expanded upside, we progressed toward our biggest hypothesis.

7. The Breakthrough: The 100X Multiplier & Player Segmentation

The defining turning point of Rocket Skill was testing a bold hypothesis: What happens if we expand the risk/reward ceiling all the way to 100X?

This wasn't simply changing a number in the database; it was architecting a complete two-tier player segmentation model:

🎮 Casual Player Cohort
Lower Entry Fees · Lower Risk · Accessible Entertainment
Casual users enjoyed frequent rounds, low-pressure wagering, and steady cashouts at 2X–5X without risking large balances.
🚀 High-Intent Player Cohort
Higher Minimum Stakes · Up to 100X Multipliers · Massive Upside
High-value players could challenge massive 50X–100X trajectories in dedicated high-stakes contests with commensurate prize pools.

The Leaderboard / Social Proof Aspiration Loop

The introduction of 100X multipliers unlocked an unexpected, powerful organic growth flywheel inside the BigCash app:

Loop
Player Hits 50X–100X Multiplier → Featured on In-Game Leaderboard → Social Proof Sparks Aspiration
When users saw fellow players turning ₹100 into ₹5,000+ on the live leaderboard, it created an authentic aspirational layer. Casual players graduated into higher-stake contests, and dormant players returned to try their skill.

9. Platform Impact: High-Frequency Engagement & Wallet Activity

Beyond Rocket Skill's standalone revenue, the game played an important strategic role in strengthening the broader BigCash ecosystem:

  • High-Frequency Engagement Loop: Because rounds lasted only 10–20 seconds, Rocket Skill served as a natural "between-games" activity for poker, rummy, or fantasy sports players waiting for tournaments to start.
  • Wallet Activity & Add-Cash Contribution: The repeat-play behavior and seamless round turnover contributed to additional wallet utilization and repeat Add Cash transactions across the platform.
  • Social Proof & Visible Aspirations: The live leaderboard celebrated real winning rounds, transforming individual high-multiplier outcomes into a platform-wide aspirational engagement layer.

11. PM Learnings & Reflection

The Fundamental Lesson in Monetization & Game Economics
"Monetization growth often comes from expanding the set of choices available to different user segments—not simply increasing prices or pushing discounts. By matching stake, risk, and potential upside to player intent, we were able to break through a ₹3L/day revenue ceiling and scale Rocket Skill to ₹8L/day."

Key Takeaways for Product Managers:

  1. Never celebrate a plateau as a permanent win: When a product reaches a healthy baseline (like ₹3L/day), it is tempting to shift focus elsewhere. Tracking DAU trends alongside revenue revealed the hidden growth saturation before it became a crisis.
  2. Combine quantitative telemetry with qualitative human calls: Data shows what is happening (fewer rounds per session); 1:1 user calls reveal why (the 10X multiplier ceiling felt limiting compared to competitors).
  3. Segment by player intent rather than demographic proxies: Giving casual players a low-barrier playground while giving high-intent players a 100X high-stakes arena allowed both cohorts to thrive without cannibalizing each other.
  4. Speed to market creates the ultimate validation loop: Shipping an MVP in 15 days gave us the real-world behavioral foundation to eventually build an ₹8L/day commercial powerhouse.
